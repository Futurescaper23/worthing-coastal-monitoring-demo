# Worthing Coastal Defence Scheme: FutureScaping client research

**Research date:** 27 August 2026  
**Scope:** Worthing Capital Maintenance Coastal Defence Project, its monitoring phase, available evidence and data, delivery responsibilities, contacts, procurement routes, and a Worthing-specific demonstration concept.

## Executive verdict

Worthing is a credible FutureScaping prospect, but the proposition needs to be narrow and technically literate.

The weak pitch is “we can fly a drone and monitor your beach”. Worthing Borough Council already hosts the regional survey team for the Beachy Head–Selsey Bill coastline, uses UAVs, RTK GNSS and mobile laser scanning, and approved the purchase of new regional coastal-monitoring laser equipment in February 2025. The council and Channel Coastal Observatory therefore already possess unusually strong capture capability.

The stronger pitch is: **turn the data they already collect into a simple, project-specific evidence and decision layer for the repaired groyne field**. This could integrate the post-works UAV baseline, spring/autumn profiles, historical elevation data, work areas and groyne references, and storm conditions into bay-by-bay change, volume and intervention views.

The timing is good. Repairs finished in July 2026; officers have identified the recent UAV survey as the monitoring starting point; the public programme anticipates monitoring before possible shingle recycling in September 2027 and 2028. The scheme is also a bridge to a much larger, long-term strategy for the whole Worthing frontage, expected to take about ten years to develop.

**Recommended first move:** approach Martyn Payne and Dan Amos together with a one-area, data-to-decision demonstration—not an offer of a free full-site survey. Ask for a short technical conversation and read-only access to the post-works UAV deliverables and latest profiles for one work area.

## 1. What the project is

The Environment Agency-led Worthing Capital Maintenance Coastal Defence Project repairs the most critical timber groynes over approximately 3.7 km, from groyne 69 at Sea View Road to groyne 140 at Brooklands. Its purpose is to extend the life of the existing defence system by up to ten years while the Environment Agency and Worthing Borough Council develop a longer-term strategy for the whole frontage.

Worthing’s primary defence is the shingle beach; the groynes retain and regulate that shingle. The intervention therefore concerns the performance of the beach-groyne system, not simply the condition of the timber structures.

The current council material records:

- approximately £5 million total scheme cost;
- £400,000 contributed by Worthing Borough Council, including £200,000 of developer contributions;
- the remainder funded through Defra Flood and Coastal Erosion Risk Management Grant-in-Aid;
- Environment Agency as scheme lead;
- VolkerStevin as main construction contractor;
- ten construction work areas between Seaview Road and Brooklands;
- structural repairs completed in July 2026, with minor defects and some missing planks to be finished later;
- a recent UAV survey to form the post-repair monitoring baseline;
- environmental enhancement work planned for autumn 2026.

There is a minor public-source discrepancy in the whole-frontage asset count. The council’s main defence description and the July 2025 committee report say 144 timber groynes and 11 rock groynes, plus three rock revetments; a FAQ lower on the same webpage says 147 timber and 10 rock groynes. Use 144/11 as the better-supported current figure, but confirm the live asset register before putting totals into a proposal or demonstrator.

### What is not publicly confirmed

As at 27 August 2026, the following were not found as clean, scheme-specific public downloads:

- the July 2026 post-works UAV DSM/DTM, point cloud, orthomosaic or processing report;
- an as-built list mapping exact repair type to every groyne;
- the design beach profiles, crest levels, minimum retained volumes or intervention thresholds;
- the six Phase 2 shingle-transfer section geometries and target volumes;
- the latest condition-assessment database and construction completion/as-built package;
- a current, project-specific monitoring report or dashboard.

These are the highest-value items to request.

## 5. Worthing-specific demonstration: what we can build before contact

### Recommended demonstrator

Build a compact **Pier East monitoring proof** centred on Area 1 and the frontage immediately east of Worthing Pier, while making clear that the final profile-to-groyne selection must be confirmed.

### Public-data-only version

The pre-contact version can contain:

1. A clean scheme map on an openly licensed basemap.
2. A historical profile panel for one or two CCO profiles.
3. A wider elevation/context panel from timestamped public LiDAR.
4. A storm and survey timeline using nearby environmental feeds.
5. A decision-screen mock-up showing intended post-works fields.
6. A provenance drawer with source, survey date, CRS, licence and quality status.

This is enough to show the interaction and evidence model. It is **not enough to claim measured post-repair performance or defensible shingle volumes**.

### The client-data unlock

Ask for:

- UAV DSM/DTM or point cloud;
- orthomosaic;
- flight date, extent, CRS, control and QC report;
- latest spring/autumn profile data and geometry;
- repaired groyne IDs and repair types;
- any target beach profile, crest or minimum-volume thresholds;
- relevant as-built sketches and six shingle-transfer section boundaries.

## 6. Commercial positioning

The likely need is not capture capability. It is turning existing surveys into a repeatable, project-specific evidence layer that helps engineers compare bays, explain change and support future intervention decisions.

## 7. Procurement and route-to-market implications

The relationship-led route should continue, but procurement routes and the regional coastal-monitoring DPS need checking before formal expansion.

## 8. Recommended action sequence

1. Register for CCO access and collect the public profile/elevation/time-series data for the Pier East candidate.
2. Confirm whether 2026 survey products and the July post-works UAV deliverables are already in the catalogue.
3. Produce the public-data demonstration shell with the post-works layer marked unavailable.
4. Retrieve or request the final MMO licence package and environmental attachments.
5. Check the current coastal-monitoring procurement route.

## 9. Risks and qualification

| Risk | Assessment | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Existing in-house capability makes the offer redundant | Material | Sell integration, interpretation and repeatability. |
| Post-works UAV data is unpublished or restricted | Likely | Build the public-data shell first and request a controlled sample. |
| Existing reporting already covers the requirement | Unknown | Ask directly what the current reporting package includes. |
| Engineering liability from simplified metrics | Material | Keep provenance, uncertainty and explicit limits visible at all times. |

## Bottom line

Worthing is worth approaching, but the demonstration has to be technically honest, narrow in scope, and clearly framed as a public-data conversation starter rather than an engineering assessment.
